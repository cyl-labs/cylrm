## Ring them at the scheduled time, never before

**Don't call early to ask if they're ready.** It gives them an easy way out ("now's not a good time"), and the demo is gone. Cal.com already sent them a reminder. Call **right on the minute**. Talk as if the demo has already started. Don't say the company name.

> **You say** Hey, is this [name]? Someone from my team talked to you last week. We set up a time for a voice agent demo. Do you remember?

If they don't answer, send a text. Then call again.

**To bring the agent in,** use the buttons on the lead's call screen:

```
Call them  →  Add call  →  pick the agent's line  →  Merge calls
```

Press **Merge calls** while the agent is still ringing. It joins the second the agent answers. The agent starts talking right away. Keep this tab open. If you close it, both calls drop.

## Before you bring the agent in

Tell them what to expect first. If you don't, they will judge a sample like it is the real thing.

> **You say** Before I bring it in, I want you to know this is a sample. For a real customer, we build it around their business. This is just so you can hear how it sounds and how it books a job. I'm going to add it to the call and ask it a few things. When I hang up, tell me what you think.

## Straight after the demo, get their numbers

Don't ask "did you like it?" You will get a vague answer, and the call gets stuck. Ask for numbers instead. Then they do the math for you.

> **You say** What did you think of how the call went? How is it different from your real calls?

_(let them answer)_

> **You say** What is a normal job worth to you, when someone calls and books?

_(let them answer)_

> **You say** About how many calls do you think you miss in a week?

_(let them answer)_

**Type each number in as they say it.** The calculator is right below. Its two boxes are these two questions. If you wait, you have to remember both numbers while you scroll.

## Do the math out loud, with their numbers

_(let them answer)_

Let the number sit. Don't say a price yet. Let them see what they are losing. Then say the package the calculator marks **cheapest**. It already fits how many calls they get. **Don't sell them a bigger one.**

## Objection handling | If they say it's too expensive

> **Prospect** That's too expensive. / That's a lot for me.

Don't argue about the price. Don't give a discount. Go back to the numbers they told you. They already said how much they lose. The only question is whether the agent saves one of those calls.

> **You say** I get it. But look at your numbers. You said you miss about [their calls] calls a week. A normal job is [their average job]. If the agent saves just one job a month, that is [their average job] coming in. The price is [the package price]. It already paid for itself.

_(let them answer)_

If a job is worth more than the price, say so.

> **You say** So it only takes one job a month. Every job after that is money you are not getting today.

_(let them answer)_

Still stuck on price? Offer the trial.

**There is no discount, on purpose.** Every plan is month to month. Either side can stop with 7 days' notice. If they ask for a lower price for paying early, say: "We keep one simple price and no long contract. The trial is how you find out if it is worth it."

## Objection handling | If they say they don't get enough calls to justify it

> **Prospect** I don't really get enough calls for this. / It's pretty quiet for me.

Don't argue about how many calls they get. Often they get few calls because people can't find them. So this is a second reason to buy. They may get fewer calls because they don't show as always open.

> **You say** Are you listed as open 24 hours on Google, by any chance?

_(let them answer)_

If they're not:

> **You say** That could be part of it. Google puts businesses that are open 24 hours a little higher on Maps. That is most true for people searching late at night. Those businesses show up near the top.

_(let them answer)_

> **You say** Once the agent is picking up, you can set your hours to 24/7 on Google, because someone will really answer. So you would likely get more calls, not just catch the ones you miss now. You can't really do that without it.

_(let them answer)_

Then go back to the package that fits the calls they do get.

The callers' objection sheets have the same answer. If you change the words here, change them there too.

## Objection handling | Only if they hesitate: the 30-day trial

This is the backup, not the opener. Use it when they heard the price and still won't move. They might say "let me think about it" or "I need to ask someone." If they did not push back on price, don't bring it up. You would be giving a discount for a problem nobody had.

It is the same 30-day trial the callers offer as a last step. So they hear the same offer here, not a better one. Make it about no risk, not about price.

> **You say** What we would normally do is let you try it for 30 days. You will see if it really picks up calls and brings in work. At the end, you decide if you want to keep it.

_(let them answer)_

If they like it but still hesitate:

> **You say** There's no real risk. I send you the agreement. It is already filled in. You will know the exact date it ends, because it goes on your calendar. If it hasn't earned its keep by then, you stop it. That's it.

_(let them answer)_

## Send the agreement while you are still on the call

Send it before you hang up. It **already has their name on it**. All they have to do is sign. You don't need to explain how it gets to them. Just say it is on its way, and send it.

> **You say** I'm sending you the agreement now. It already has your name on it. All you have to do is sign.

_(send it)_

Then walk them through it. Don't ask them to read it alone. You are on the phone, so it takes five minutes, and they can ask you anything.

> **You say** Since we're on the phone, let's go through it together. Open it up and I'll show you each part.

_(let them answer)_

**For the paid agreement, go in this order.** Use the number they see on the page for each part.

1. **Who it is for.** "This part is you and us. It has your business name. It is already filled in."
2. **What we do.** "We answer the calls you miss. We book the jobs. You get an email about each call."
3. **Price and minutes.** "This is your monthly price. It covers this many minutes of calls each month. If you go over, each extra minute costs this much."
4. **How long it lasts.** "It goes month to month. There is no long contract. You can stop any time. We just need 7 days' notice."
5. **Sign.** "Draw your name with your finger, or type it. That's all."

**For the trial agreement, go in this order.**

1. **Who it is for.** Same as above.
2. **What the trial is.** "You get 30 days, or up to 75 minutes of calls, whichever comes first. It only covers the calls you miss."
3. **What it costs.** "It is $1 to start."
4. **What happens at the end.** "After that, you decide. Keep it, or stop. If you want more minutes, they are $1 each."
5. **Sign.** "Draw your name or type it. That's all."

If they want to read it on their own first, that is fine. Don't push. You have the next call booked anyway.

> **You say** No problem. It's yours to keep. Read it over and send it back when you're ready.

## Then the questions, and a call to hear it working

Two things come after the signature. The first is the questions you need from them. The second is the day they hear it working. Say the second one second, but it is what makes them answer the first. Nobody fills in questions for fun.

> **You say** Once that's done, I'll send you a short list of questions about your business. Like how you want it to answer and what you want it to ask people. That's what I build your agent from.

_(let them answer)_

> **You say** Send those back to me and give me two or three days. Let's pick a time now, and I'll show it to you, so you can hear it answer as your business.

_(let them answer)_

Never end on "I'll follow up." Offer two times on two different days, the same way the callers are taught.

> **You say** What does your schedule look like? I have Thursday afternoon, or we could do Friday morning.

**Going fully live takes about a week. That is a different promise from the call above.** The call in two or three days is to show them the agent with their answers in it. Going live means setting up call forwarding, running test calls, and getting it to answer the way they want. That takes about a week. Only say "a week" if they ask when it will be live. Never say it first. If you say a week while you are booking a call for Thursday, it sounds like a contradiction, and you lose the momentum you just built.
