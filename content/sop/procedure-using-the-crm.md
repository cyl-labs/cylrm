Everything here applies whichever region you call. Times shown anywhere in the
app are **Singapore time**.

## You only see your own niches

Call lists are assigned. The dialler, the spreadsheet, the pipeline and the
callbacks diary all show you the lists that are yours and nothing else, so if a
niche is missing it has not been assigned to you yet — ask rather than assume
it is empty.

## The queue decides the order, not you

The dialler hands you one lead at a time, in this order:

- callbacks that are due now
- leads nobody has ever rung
- everything else, oldest attempt first

You can jump the queue by picking someone out of **Up next**, and the order
resumes where it was afterwards.

## Calls are placed on your handset

The big button copies the number to your clipboard — it does not dial. Paste it
into your phone or softphone, make the call, then log what happened.

## Log every call, including the ones nobody answered

Pick the outcome, then press the confirm button underneath. Nothing is written
until you confirm, so a mis-tap costs nothing as long as you have not confirmed
yet.

What the outcomes mean:

- **No answer** — rang out, nobody picked up.
- **Voicemail** — went to voicemail.
- **Gatekeeper** — you reached a person, but not one who can decide anything.
- **Call back** — they asked to be rung at a particular time.
- **Demo booked** — they agreed to a slot. See *After you book a meeting*.
- **Not interested** — a clear no from someone who can give one.
- **Bad number** — dead line, wrong business, or not a real number.

**Trial**, **won** and **lost** are not on the dialler on purpose. They happen
days or weeks after the call, and are set from the pipeline board or the
spreadsheet.

## A booked callback leaves the queue until it is due

Choosing **Call back** asks for a time and defaults to tomorrow morning. That
lead then drops out of the queue and comes back when the time arrives — it also
appears in **Callbacks**, which is the diary for everyone you owe a call,
overdue first, across every niche.

## Logging again is a second dial. Correcting is not.

These are different actions and the numbers depend on the difference:

- **Ringing someone again** — log it from the dialler as normal. That is a new
  attempt, and the try count goes up.
- **Fixing an outcome you tapped wrongly** — change it from the **category**
  cell on the Spreadsheet, or the card menu on the Pipeline. That overwrites
  the last call instead of adding one, so a mis-tap does not read as a dial
  that never happened.

## Skip versus log

**Skip without logging** takes the lead out of your queue for this session only
— nothing is recorded and it comes back later. Use it when you were not ready,
not when the call happened.

## Notes

Whatever you type is kept against that call and shown to whoever picks the lead
up next, including you in three weeks. Write the thing you would want to know
before ringing again: who you spoke to, what they said, when to try.

## Objection handling, mid-call

The **Objection handling** button on the dial card opens the sheet over the top
of the dialler without leaving the page — or press **O**. Search it for what
they just said. It shows the version for the country you are calling.
