**Founders only.** This is the follow-up demo call, where you bring the AI
receptionist onto the line and play a customer while the owner listens in. It
is not part of the cold call and callers never run it. Each script is one
realistic call from start to finish: the details only that business has, plus
a curveball where the script has one. It should take 5 to 7 minutes.

Use the general template for any new client. The two worked examples (First
Coast Alarm and Next Level Haul Away) show what a filled-in script looks like.
After every demo, do the two parts at the bottom: go through each service's
questions, then sort out call forwarding.

## Say this before you dial the agent in

> **You say** I'm going to add your receptionist to the call and play one of your customers. Go ahead and mute, and just listen like you're overhearing your front desk. It'll save a real request, and I'll clear it out afterwards.

- **Owner on mute.** The agent hears everyone on the conference and will answer any voice it picks up.
- **Talk like a normal customer:** a little casual, the odd "um". A polished, rehearsed call sounds staged.
- **Give the name Mark** and the email cyllabsdigital@gmail.com, spelled out clearly when the agent asks.
- **When the call ends,** drop the agent, ask the owner to unmute, then ask the debrief question.

## General template

Use it for any new client. Fill in the bracketed parts from their Services and Service questions pages. Pick their most common job, the call 80% of their customers make. It ends with a saved booking, quote or request, depending on how the service is priced.

### 1. Opening

> **You say** Hi, um, yeah, I was calling about [common job, in plain words]. Is that something you guys do?

**Agent should:** greet with the business name, confirm the service, start the questions.

**Owner hears:** their own business name answering the phone.

### 2. Local check

> **You say** And you cover [a real town in their service area], right?

**Agent should:** confirm it from the service area.

### 3. Price question

> **You say** Roughly how much does that run?

**Agent should:** answer the way the owner prices it: a fixed price, a range, or "the team will quote you".

**Owner hears:** their own pricing rule. This is the part owners are most nervous about.

### 4. Their questions

Answer each of the service's questions naturally, one at a time: [question 1], [question 2], and so on.

**Agent should:** ask one question at a time, in the order on the Service questions page.

### 5. Curveball: something only they get asked

Pick one: change an answer ("actually, make it next week"), ask about something they don't offer, or ask about a policy that is in their Documents.

**Agent should:** handle it without losing its place in the questions.

### 6. Name and email

> **You say** It's Mark. Email is cyllabsdigital@gmail.com.

**Agent should:** read the email back, letter by letter where it matters.

### 7. Summary and close

> **You say** Yep, that's all correct. ... No, that's it, thanks.

**Agent should:** read back a summary, save it, say what happens next, then end the call politely.

**Owner hears:** a clean handoff: "the team will reach out to..."

**Listen for**

- Correct business name and services
- Pricing said exactly the way the owner wants it
- One question at a time, no repeats
- Email read back correctly

**Avoid on a demo**

- Asking for a real person, or anything in their transfer rules. The transfer goes to the escalation number.
- Talking over the agent while it is saving.

**Debrief question**

> **You say** How did that sound? Anything it said that you wouldn't say to a customer?

## Example: First Coast Alarm

**Caller:** Mark, facilities manager of a small office building off Baymeadows Rd, Jacksonville. **Service:** site walk (security, takeover or add-on). **Ends with:** a quote request saved. No appointment is booked; the team follows up to set the walk.

### 1. Opening

> **You say** Hi, yeah, I manage a small office building off Baymeadows. The previous tenant left an old camera system and we want to upgrade it, maybe add card readers on the doors too.

**Agent should:** greet as First Coast Alarm and steer toward a site walk.

**Owner hears:** it understands a commercial security job straight away.

### 2. Price question

> **You say** Ballpark, what are we looking at for cameras? And the door readers?

**Agent should:** give ranges only: about $500 per camera installed, $2,000 to $2,500 per door for access control. No firm total; the final number comes from the site walk.

**Owner hears:** their real ranges and no firm price. This matches their "price ranges only" rule.

### 3. Monitoring

> **You say** Do you do monitoring too? We're kind of stuck in a contract with our current company.

**Agent should:** say business burglar monitoring is $40 a month, month to month, no contract.

### 4. Site walk questions

Answer in this order:

- System: "Security."
- Job type: "I guess a takeover, it's someone else's old system."
- Building: "Baymeadows Commons."
- Needs: "About eight cameras, readers on the two main doors, three floors."
- Timing: "Next Tuesday or Wednesday morning."

**Agent should:** ask which system, then what kind of job, then the business or building name, then what you need, then the best day and time. It should work out "Takeover or add-on" from what you say.

### 5. Name and email

> **You say** Mark. Email's cyllabsdigital@gmail.com.

**Agent should:** read the email back and confirm it.

### 6. Summary

> **You say** Yep, that's right.

**Agent should:** summarise (Security, takeover, Baymeadows Commons, 8 cameras and 2 doors, Tue/Wed morning), save the request, say the team will reach out to schedule the walk, then ask if there is anything else.

### 7. Curveball: "Anything else?" and the residential rule

> **You say** Actually, yeah. Can you guys do the alarm at my house too?

**Agent should:** explain that residential alarm services moved to their sister company, Vie Home Services, and point him there. The site walk request stays saved.

**Owner hears:** the Crystal Cove and referral rule they gave you, applied correctly. Nobody else's agent knows this.

### 8. Close

> **You say** Got it. No, that's everything, thanks.

**Agent should:** say goodbye and end the call politely.

**Listen for**

- Ranges, never a total ("so about $6,000?" should get a range plus the site walk)
- Month-to-month monitoring mentioned correctly
- Vie Home Services named on the residential question
- It says it is a request to schedule the walk, not a booked time

**Avoid on this call**

- Saying you live in Crystal Cove or were referred. That flips the residential rule.
- Asking for the sales line or a person. The transfer goes to the escalation number, which is still the test number.

**Debrief question**

> **You say** Is that how you'd want a property manager handled? Anything about the pricing or the residential answer you'd change?

## Example: Next Level Haul Away

**Caller:** Mark, homeowner clearing out a garage in Orange Park after a move. **Service:** cleanouts (garage, one room). **Ends with:** a quote request saved. The team follows up with a price.

### 1. Opening

> **You say** Hey, so we just moved and the garage is full of stuff we don't want. Old furniture, boxes, some broken shelving. Can you guys come haul it?

**Agent should:** greet as Next Level Haul Away and pick up that it is a cleanout.

### 2. Trust check

> **You say** Are you guys licensed and insured? Last company we used was kind of sketchy.

**Agent should:** say yes, licensed and insured, locally owned since 2021, not a franchise.

**Owner hears:** their own FAQ answers in a natural voice.

### 3. Price question

> **You say** How much does something like that usually cost?

**Agent should:** give no number. Explain that it depends on volume, access and disposal, that the team will quote it, and that photos help.

**Owner hears:** no made-up price, and they get the job details to quote from.

### 4. Cleanout questions

Answer in this order:

- Address: a street in Orange Park, 32073.
- Space: "The garage."
- Amount: "Probably about one room's worth."
- Timing: "Saturday morning if you can."

**Agent should:** ask the address, then the kind of space, then roughly how much, then the best day and time.

### 5. Name and email

> **You say** Mark. Email's cyllabsdigital@gmail.com.

**Agent should:** read the email back and confirm it.

### 6. Summary and close

> **You say** Yep, sounds right. ... That's it, thanks.

**Agent should:** summarise (garage cleanout, about one room, Orange Park, Saturday morning), save the quote request, and say the team will follow up with pricing.

**Bonus:** if it ends on "Let us do your dirty work for you!", owners love hearing their slogan.

**Listen for**

- It never gives a dollar amount
- Orange Park accepted as in the area

**Avoid on this call**

- "Whole house", "estate", "commercial" or a big job. Their special instructions transfer those to the team, which right now means the test escalation number.
- Mentioning damage, a complaint, or a crew already on site. Those also transfer.

**Debrief question**

> **You say** Is that how you'd answer the price question? Would you want it to ask for photos, or leave that to your team?

## After the call, part 1: go through each service's questions

Go one service at a time. Read out the questions, then ask whether anything should be added, removed or reworded. **Note every change.**

> **You say** Before we wrap up, let's go through what it asks for each of your services. For [service name], it asks...

After each one:

> **You say** Anything you'd add, take out or say differently for that one?

**Every service ends with name and email**, so you only read the questions before that. For a new client, list every service on their Services page. The two clients above, as a guide:

- **First Coast Alarm, Site walk** (quote, for new projects, takeovers and add-ons): which system is this about (Fire, Security, Network, AV); what kind of job (New construction, Takeover or add-on, Inspection); the name of the business or building; what you need, anything the team should know before they come out; what day and time would work best for the site walk.
- **First Coast Alarm, Service call** (request, for existing customers): which system is affected (Fire, Security, Network, AV); what is happening with the system; which business or building; a good day and time for a technician.
- **Next Level Haul Away, Junk Removal** (quote): the address (street, city and ZIP); what items are being hauled; where they are (Curbside, Inside ground floor, Inside with stairs); best day and time.
- **Next Level Haul Away, Cleanouts** (quote): the address; what kind of space (Garage, Whole house, Estate, Rental turnover); roughly how much (A few items, One room, Several rooms, Whole property); best day and time.
- **Next Level Haul Away, Furniture Delivery** (quote): where it is being picked up from; the delivery address; what is being delivered; ground floor, stairs or elevator at the delivery address; best day and time.
- **Next Level Haul Away, Appliance Haul and Deliver** (quote): the address; delivery, removal or both; which appliances; best day and time.
- **Next Level Haul Away, Assembly** (quote): the address; what needs assembling and how many pieces; best day and time.
- **Next Level Haul Away, Fence and Shed Removal** (quote): the address; fence, shed or both; size and material (fence length in feet, or shed dimensions); best day and time.
- **Next Level Haul Away, Donation Pickup** (quote): the address; what is being donated and its condition; curbside or inside; best day and time.

## After the call, part 2: call forwarding

Ask how they want calls routed **before** you mention the guide, because the guide depends on the answer.

### 1. All calls or missed calls

> **You say** Do you want it answering every call that comes in, or only the ones you don't get to?

- **All calls:** every call forwards straight to the receptionist.
- **Missed calls only:** their phone rings first, and it forwards after no answer or when they are busy.
- **Unsure:** suggest missed calls first. They can switch to all calls later.

### 2. Ring time

Missed calls only:

> **You say** How many rings before it picks up? Most people go with about four or five.

### 3. Phone provider

> **You say** And who's your phone provider for that number?

**Write it down.** The forwarding steps are different for each provider.

### 4. Close

> **You say** Perfect. I'll send you a short guide on setting up call forwarding for your line. Once that's done, your receptionist is live. I'll also check in about a week after it's live and we'll go over the first calls together.
