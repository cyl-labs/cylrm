title: Reporting in Slack
---

The CRM records what happened. Slack is where anyone actually sees it. Three
things are posted there and nothing else is expected of you: your numbers at
the end of the day, a booking the moment you make one, and time off before you
take it.

## Post your numbers at the end of every day

Channel: **#daily-reports**. Every caller, every day you work, at the end of
your calling session. Same day, not the next morning.

```
[Name] - [Date]
Calls made: __
Pickups: __
Meetings booked: __
```

This is not paperwork for its own sake. Three numbers a day is what makes a
drop show up in a week instead of a month, and a bad week you flagged yourself
is a conversation about the list. A bad month nobody noticed is a conversation
about you.

If you did not call at all on a working day, say so in one line rather than
posting nothing. Silence reads as a missing report, not as a quiet day.

## The post is written for you on Call lists

Do not count anything on paper. Open **Call lists** and the card at the top of
the screen is your report, already filled in, with a button that copies it
ready to paste into Slack. It appears once you have logged your first call of
the day and counts only your own calls.

What the three numbers mean:

- **Calls** is every call you logged, attempts and all.
- **Pickups** is the ones where you got a person: gatekeeper, callback, not
  interested, demo booked, and anything further down the pipeline. No answer,
  voicemail and bad number are not pickups.
- **Meetings** is demos booked, counted once per business.

They are the same figures your **Scoreboard** shows on Today, counted the same
way, so the two can never disagree.

Two things to know. The card says which clock the day was judged in, so if that
is not the one you work by, set it once on the Scoreboard and it will follow
you. And the numbers only count what you **logged**. A call you made and never
logged is a call that did not happen, here and on your pay.

## Post a booking the moment you make it

Channel: **#meetings-booked**. Posted by whoever booked it, during the calling
session, not saved up for the daily report.

```
[Name] booked a meeting!
Lead: __
Date/time of meeting: __
Trial offered: Yes / No
```

Log the call as **Demo booked** and the dialler puts this post on the screen
with the business already in it, so it is a copy, a paste and two words typed.
It stays there while you finish on Cal.com and while you move on to the next
number, and it goes when you say you have posted it. The time and the trial
answer are left blank because only you know them at that moment.

The point is that it lands while it is still news. The booking email and the
Meetings screen both reach us on their own, so this is not how we find out the
meeting exists. It is how the rest of the floor sees a win on the day it
happens instead of buried in a summary that evening.

It replaces nothing. Log the call first, leave the Cal.com notes box alone, and
the meeting still has to be confirmed the day before like any other. See
*After you book a meeting*.

## Time off is a direct message, in advance

Not the shared channel. **Direct message**, as far ahead as you can manage,
before the day in question.

Advance notice is the whole ask. A day off that is known about a week early is
planned around. The same day off found out about that morning is a niche nobody
called and callbacks nobody kept.

If something happens on the day and you cannot work, message as early in the
day as you can. That is not the same thing as notice, but it is the difference
between a gap and a surprise.
